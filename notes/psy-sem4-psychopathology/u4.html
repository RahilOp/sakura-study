Psychotic, Mood and Personality Disorders

Psychotic Disorders

  • Psychosis = loss of contact with reality; hallucinations and/or delusions.

Schizophrenia

  • Symptoms:
  • Positive symptoms = hallucinations, delusions, disorganised speech, disorganised behaviour.
  • Negative symptoms = flat affect, alogia (poverty of speech), avolition, social withdrawal.
  • Subtypes (DSM-IV): paranoid, disorganised, catatonic, undifferentiated, residual.
  • Causes: genetic vulnerability, dopamine imbalance, environmental stressors.

Delusional Disorder

  • Non-bizarre delusions lasting at least 1 month.
  • Functioning is otherwise relatively preserved.

Mood (Affective) Disorders

  • Disorders characterised by disturbances in mood.

Major Depressive Episode

  • Persistent sadness, loss of interest, fatigue, guilt, sleep/appetite changes, suicidal thoughts.
  • Lasts at least 2 weeks.

Manic Episode

  • Elevated, expansive or irritable mood.
  • Increased energy, decreased sleep, grandiosity, risky behaviour.

Bipolar Disorder

  • Alternating episodes of mania and depression.
  • Bipolar I = full manic episodes.
  • Bipolar II = hypomanic episodes plus major depressive episodes.

Dysthymia / Persistent Depressive Disorder

  • Chronic low mood lasting at least 2 years in adults.

Personality Disorders

  • Inflexible, maladaptive patterns of thinking, feeling and behaving.
  • Long-standing and causing distress or impairment.
Disorder Key traits
Dissociative Disconnection from self, memory or identity
Impulsive Acting without thinking, emotional instability
Anti-social Disregard for others' rights, deceitfulness
Borderline Unstable relationships, self-image, emotions
Avoidant Social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy
Dependent Excessive need to be taken care of
