# Introduction to Psychopathology

## Normality and Abnormality

- **Normality** = behaviour that is socially acceptable, adaptive and statistically common.
- **Abnormality** = behaviour that causes distress, impairs functioning, is socially deviant or maladaptive.
- Criteria used to judge abnormality: personal distress, disability/dysfunction, violation of social norms, statistical rarity.

## Classification of Mental Disorders

- **ICD-10** = International Classification of Diseases, 10th edition (WHO).
- **DSM-IV / DSM-5** = Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (APA).
- Both provide standard criteria, labels and codes for mental disorders.

## Signs, Symptoms and Syndromes

- **Sign** = observable indicator (e.g., trembling, rapid speech).
- **Symptom** = subjective report by the patient (e.g., feeling sad).
- **Syndrome** = a group of symptoms that occur together and characterise a disorder.

## Causes of Abnormal Behaviour

- **Biological** = genetics, brain chemistry, hormonal imbalance, brain injury.
- **Psychological** = maladaptive learning, irrational thoughts, unconscious conflicts.
- **Social** = family discord, poverty, discrimination, trauma.
- **Cultural** = cultural norms shape what is considered abnormal and how symptoms are expressed.

## Models of Psychopathology

| Model | Main idea | Key focus |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Biological | Mental disorders have physical causes | Brain, genes, neurotransmitters |
| Psychodynamic | Unconscious conflicts from childhood | Freud: id, ego, superego |
| Behavioural | Abnormal behaviour is learned | Conditioning, reinforcement |
| Cognitive-behavioural | Faulty thinking causes distress | Irrational beliefs, schemas |
| Humanistic | Blocked self-actualisation | Rogers, Maslow |
| Existential | Struggle with meaning, freedom, death | Frankl, Yalom |
| Socio-cultural | Society and culture cause disorder | Labeling, social stressors |
