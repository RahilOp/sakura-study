Poetry II

New Literatures in English: poetry

  • This unit introduces poets from New Literatures in English: Chile, Canada, Australia.
  • "New Literatures" refers to writing in English from former colonies and non-British cultures.
  • These poets address themes of love, politics, identity, landscape, history, and language.
  • Exam focus: identify the poet's nationality, central theme, and poetic style.

Pablo Neruda — If You Forget Me

Context

  • Pablo Neruda was a Chilean poet, diplomat, and Nobel laureate.
  • He is one of the most widely read poets in Spanish; his work is often political and romantic.

Theme

  • "If You Forget Me" is a love poem with an unusual condition.
  • The speaker says he will forget the beloved if she forgets him first.
  • But if she remains faithful, his love will be boundless and rooted.

Key features

  • Direct, intimate address.
  • Imagery of roots, fire, and daily life.
  • Blends personal love with political undertones (Neruda wrote it during exile).

Exam points

  • Neruda is Chilean.
  • The poem is a conditional love poem.
  • It uses imagery of rootedness and commitment.

Margaret Atwood — Spellings

Context

  • Margaret Atwood is a Canadian poet, novelist, and critic.
  • Her work often explores gender, power, language, and environmental issues.

Theme

  • "Spellings" reflects on how language shapes reality and identity.
  • It connects spelling/words with the power to name, define, and control.
  • Feminist concern: women have historically been denied the power of language and naming.

Style

  • Witty, ironic, precise.
  • Uses wordplay and double meanings.
  • Concise and epigrammatic.

Exam points

  • Atwood is Canadian.
  • The poem links language, naming, and power.
  • Feminist and postmodern concerns are central.

Patrick White — Voss

Context

  • Patrick White was an Australian novelist and playwright; he won the Nobel Prize in Literature.
  • Voss is a novel, but the poem/syllabus selection relates to his exploration of the Australian interior and spiritual quest.

Theme

  • The title refers to Johann Ulrich Voss, a German explorer crossing the Australian desert.
  • The work explores obsession, colonial exploration, myth, and the Australian landscape.
  • White often writes about spiritual isolation and the failure of European settlers to understand the land.

Exam points

  • Patrick White is Australian.
  • Voss deals with exploration, the Australian interior, and spiritual quest.
  • Themes: isolation, colonial encounter, landscape as symbol.

Judith Wright — The Company of Lovers

Context

  • Judith Wright was an Australian poet, environmentalist, and activist for Aboriginal rights.
  • Her poetry combines personal love with awareness of history and landscape.

Theme

  • "The Company of Lovers" celebrates love while acknowledging its vulnerability.
  • The lovers are part of a larger company across time; love is both private and universal.
  • There is often an elegiac awareness of mortality and loss.

Style

  • Lyrical, meditative.
  • Connects private emotion with public/historical consciousness.

Exam points

  • Judith Wright is Australian.
  • The poem explores love, mortality, and human connection.
  • She is also known for nature and Aboriginal-rights themes.
