# Prose I

## Mahatma Gandhi — Hind Swaraj

### Context
- **Hind Swaraj** (Indian Home Rule) was written by **Mahatma Gandhi** in 1909.
- It is a dialogue between the Reader and the Editor, written originally in Gujarati.
- Gandhi criticises modern Western civilisation and offers a vision of self-rule rooted in Indian values.

### Core ideas
- **Swaraj** means more than political independence; it means self-rule at personal and national levels.
- True swaraj requires moral and spiritual discipline.
- Gandhi rejects violence, industrial materialism, and parliamentary politics copied from the West.

### Chapter XVII: Passive Resistance
- Also called **Satyagraha**.
- It is a method of non-violent resistance to unjust laws.
- Requires courage, self-sacrifice, and truth.
- Different from cowardice and from violent rebellion.
- Key requirement: the resister must be willing to suffer rather than retaliate.

### Chapter XVIII: Education
- Gandhi critiques English education as overly literary and foreign.
- True education should develop character, practical skills, and moral sense.
- He prefers education in the mother tongue and through craft or manual work.
- The aim is to make a person self-reliant and socially useful.

### Exam points
- *Hind Swaraj* was written in **1909**.
- Gandhi's ideal: self-rule based on simplicity, non-violence, and Indian values.
- Passive resistance = **satyagraha**.
- Gandhi criticises Western civilisation, machinery worship, and colonial education.

## Urvashi Butalia — The Other Side of Silence

### Context
- **Urvashi Butalia** is an Indian feminist publisher and writer.
- *The Other Side of Silence* is an oral history of the **Partition of India** in 1947.
- It recovers voices of women, children, and ordinary people often left out of official histories.

### Chapter "Memory"
- The chapter explores how Partition is remembered through individual and collective memory.
- Women's experiences of abduction, violence, displacement, and recovery are central.
- Memory is selective, painful, and shaped by family and community silence.

### Key themes
- **Trauma and silence**: families often hid Partition experiences.
- **Gendered violence**: women suffered specific forms of violence and stigma.
- **Recovery and rehabilitation**: governments and families struggled to recover abducted women.
- **Oral history as method**: personal testimony challenges written, official accounts.

### Exam points
- Butalia's work is an **oral history of Partition**.
- It focuses on women's voices and memories.
- Memory is a key theme: official history vs. lived experience.

## Comparing the two prose texts

| Text | Author | Focus | Method/Style |
|------|--------|-------|--------------|
| *Hind Swaraj* | Gandhi | Self-rule, non-violence, education | Philosophical dialogue |
| *The Other Side of Silence* | Urvashi Butalia | Partition, memory, gender | Oral history, narrative |

### Exam checklist
- Gandhi: *Hind Swaraj*, 1909, satyagraha, critique of Western civilisation.
- Butalia: oral history of Partition, women's voices, memory and silence.
