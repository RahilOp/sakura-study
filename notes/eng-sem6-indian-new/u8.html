Drama II

New Literatures in English: drama

  • This unit covers playwrights from Nigeria and Canada.
  • Both plays blend traditional performance elements with modern dramatic forms.
  • Exam focus: identify the playwright, cultural context, dramatic technique, and central conflict.

Wole Soyinka — The Lion and the Jewel

Context

  • Wole Soyinka is a Nigerian playwright, poet, and Nobel laureate.
  • The Lion and the Jewel (1959) is one of his most popular plays.
  • It is set in a Yoruba village and explores the clash between tradition and modernity.

Plot

  • Sidi, the village beauty, is pursued by two men:
  • Baroka, the elderly Bale (chief), representing tradition.
  • Lakunle, the young schoolteacher, representing Western modernity.
  • Baroka tricks Sidi into marriage, showing the persistence of traditional power.

Themes

  • Tradition vs modernity: Lakunle's Western ideas fail against Baroka's cunning.
  • Gender and power: Sidi's beauty makes her an object of competition.
  • Theatrical fusion: Yoruba music, dance, and mime blend with English drama.
  • Postcolonial critique: Soyinka is sceptical of uncritical Westernisation.

Dramatic technique

  • Use of Yoruba griot/storyteller traditions.
  • Dance, drumming, and mime are integral to the action.
  • Comedy and satire.

Exam points

  • Soyinka is Nigerian and a Nobel laureate.
  • The Lion and the Jewel is a comedy of tradition vs modernity.
  • Characters: Sidi, Baroka (the lion), Lakunle.
  • Baroka wins, symbolising the strength of tradition.

Trey Anthony — 'da Kink in My Hair

Context

  • Trey Anthony is a Canadian playwright and actor of Jamaican descent.
  • 'da Kink in My Hair is a play about Black Canadian women's lives.
  • It began as a successful stage play and later became a television series.

Plot and theme

  • The play is set in a hair salon in Toronto's Jamaican community.
  • Various women come to the salon, sharing stories about identity, love, racism, immigration, and family.
  • The salon becomes a space of community, confession, and empowerment.

Key concerns

  • Black Canadian identity: experiences of immigration, colourism, and belonging.
  • Women's voices: the play centres women's stories and friendships.
  • Hair as symbol: hair represents identity, beauty standards, cultural pride, and social pressure.

Dramatic technique

  • Multiple monologues and interconnected stories.
  • Use of dialect and Caribbean-inflected English.
  • Comic and emotional shifts.

Exam points

  • Trey Anthony is a Canadian playwright of Jamaican origin.
  • 'da Kink in My Hair is set in a Toronto hair salon.
  • Themes: Black womanhood, immigration, identity, community.
  • Hair is a central symbol.
