# Prose II

## Postcolonial and feminist prose

- This unit brings together two influential thinkers: a postcolonial psychologist and an Indian feminist theorist.
- Both texts examine how power shapes identity: race in Fanon, gender in Menon.
- Exam focus: identify the key arguments and their relevance to literary/cultural studies.

---

## Frantz Fanon — Black Skin, White Masks

### Context
- **Frantz Fanon** was a psychiatrist and political philosopher from Martinique.
- *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952) is a foundational text of postcolonial theory.
- It analyses the psychological effects of colonialism and racism.

### Chapter 4: key ideas
- Fanon examines the lived experience of being Black in a white-dominated world.
- He describes how colonial ideology creates **inferiority complexes** and self-hatred among the colonised.
- The colonised subject is forced to see himself through the coloniser's eyes.

### Key concepts
- **Internalisation of inferiority**: colonised people adopt the values and judgments of the coloniser.
- **Alienation**: racial domination splits the self and blocks authentic identity.
- **Language and identity**: speaking the coloniser's language can mean accepting the coloniser's world view.
- **Resistance**: Fanon ultimately calls for political struggle and psychological liberation.

### Exam points
- Fanon is central to **postcolonial studies**.
- *Black Skin, White Masks* studies racism's psychological damage.
- Key terms: internalisation, alienation, colonial gaze, resistance.

## Nivedita Menon — Seeing Like a Feminist

### Context
- **Nivedita Menon** is an Indian feminist theorist and political thinker.
- *Seeing Like a Feminist* introduces feminist ideas for a general reader.
- Chapter 2 focuses on key feminist debates and concepts.

### Key feminist ideas
- **Patriarchy**: a social system in which men hold primary power.
- **Gender**: a social construction, not simply biological sex.
- **Intersectionality**: gender intersects with caste, class, religion, and sexuality.
- **Public/private divide**: feminist politics challenges the idea that family and household are outside politics.

### Chapter 2 focus
- Menon discusses how feminism questions apparently natural institutions like family, marriage, and sexuality.
- She argues that the personal is political.
- Feminist politics is not only about women's rights but about transforming social relations.

### Exam points
- Menon is an Indian feminist theorist.
- "Seeing like a feminist" means questioning gendered power in everyday life.
- Key concepts: patriarchy, gender as social construct, intersectionality, personal is political.

## Comparing Fanon and Menon

| Thinker | Text | Focus | Core argument |
|---------|------|-------|---------------|
| Frantz Fanon | *Black Skin, White Masks* | Race and colonialism | Colonialism damages the psyche; liberation requires resistance |
| Nivedita Menon | *Seeing Like a Feminist* | Gender and patriarchy | Gender power shapes society; feminism questions the "natural" order |

### Exam checklist
- Fanon: colonialism, racism, alienation, postcolonial theory.
- Menon: feminism, patriarchy, gender as construction, intersectionality.
