# Eliot, Yeats, Larkin

## Period overview: 20th-century poetry

- This unit covers **Modernist** and **post-war** British poetry.
- 20th-century poetry breaks traditional forms, experiments with voice, and responds to urban life, war, doubt, and social change.
- Exam focus: identify the movement, the speaker's attitude, and the poem's central anxiety.

### Modernism vs. The Movement
- **Modernism** (early 20th c.): fragmentation, allusion, urban alienation, mythic method. Eliot and Yeats are key figures.
- **The Movement** (1950s): plainer language, irony, everyday subjects, scepticism. Philip Larkin is a leading example.

## T. S. Eliot — The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock

### Context
- One of the most important **Modernist poems**.
- First published in **1915**; introduced a new, fragmented style to English poetry.
- The speaker is a middle-aged man paralysed by self-consciousness.

### Form
- **Dramatic interior monologue**; free verse with irregular rhythm.
- Allusions to Shakespeare, Dante, the Bible, and mythology.

### Main themes
- **Indecision and paralysis**: Prufrock cannot act, cannot declare love, cannot even eat a peach without anxiety.
- **Urban alienation**: the modern city is sterile and oppressive.
- **Aging and missed opportunity**: "I have measured out my life with coffee spoons."

### Key images
- The evening "spread out against the sky / Like a patient etherized upon a table."
- "Do I dare?" and "Do I dare?" — repeated self-questioning.
- Mermaids singing but not to Prufrock.

### Exam points
- Eliot was a leading **Modernist** poet.
- *Prufrock* is an interior monologue about **paralysis and alienation**.
- Famous phrase: "I have measured out my life with coffee spoons."

## W. B. Yeats — The Lake Isle of Innisfree

### Context
- Yeats was an Irish poet associated with the **Irish Literary Revival**.
- The poem expresses a desire to escape modern urban life to a small island in Ireland.

### Form
- **Lyric**; regular three-line stanzas; musical, almost ballad-like.
- Uses repetition and sensory imagery.

### Main theme
- **Escape to nature**: the speaker longs for a simple cabin, bean-rows, and honeybees.
- The isle represents peace, independence, and spiritual renewal.

### Key images
- "Nine bean-rows will I have there."
- "Peace comes dropping slow."
- The lake water "lapping with low sounds by the shore."

### Exam points
- Yeats is an **Irish** poet and Nobel laureate.
- *The Lake Isle of Innisfree* is a **Romantic/escapist lyric**.
- It contrasts urban London with the imagined peace of Innisfree.

## Philip Larkin — Church Going

### Context
- Larkin was a leading poet of **The Movement** in post-war Britain.
- His poetry is known for plain language, irony, and scepticism.

### Form
- **Dramatic lyric**: a speaker bicycling into a country church, then reflecting on it.
- Conversational tone with rhymed stanzas.

### Main theme
- The decline of organised religion and what remains of it.
- The church still matters as a place of seriousness, marriage, death, and community memory.
- The speaker is unsure whether the church is a "serious house on serious earth" or just a relic.

### Key questions
- What will happen when churches fall completely out of use?
- Will people still feel a need for such places of ritual and meaning?

### Exam points
- Larkin represents **The Movement**.
- *Church Going* explores the **decline of religion** in modern England.
- Tone: ironic, sceptical, yet respectful.

## 20th-century comparison

| Poem | Poet | Movement | Key concern |
|------|------|----------|-------------|
| *The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock* | T. S. Eliot | Modernism | Urban alienation, indecision |
| *The Lake Isle of Innisfree* | W. B. Yeats | Irish Revival / early Modernism | Escape to nature |
| *Church Going* | Philip Larkin | The Movement | Decline of religion |

### Exam checklist
- Eliot = Modernism, fragmentation, urban paralysis.
- Yeats = Irish poet, longing for Innisfree, nature and escape.
- Larkin = The Movement, irony, decline of church/religion.
