# Stanza forms

## What is a stanza?

- A **stanza** is a grouped set of lines in a poem, like a paragraph in prose.
- Stanzas create rhythm, emphasis, and logical units.
- The same stanza form repeated throughout a poem gives it structure.
- Exam focus: name the form, identify its line count and rhyme scheme, give an example.

## Two-line and unrhymed forms

### Heroic couplet
- Two rhyming lines in **iambic pentameter**.
- "Heroic" because it was used for epic/elevated subjects.
- Common in **Neoclassical** poetry: Dryden, Pope, Johnson.
- Effect: concise, witty, epigrammatic.
- Example: much of Pope's *Essay on Man*.

### Blank verse
- **Unrhymed iambic pentameter**.
- Closest English equivalent to classical epic metre.
- Flexible and dignified; used in drama and long poems.
- Famous users: **Milton** (*Paradise Lost*), **Shakespeare** (plays), **Wordsworth** (*The Prelude*).
- Exam clue: "unrhymed iambic pentameter" = blank verse.

## Spenserian stanza and terza rima

### Spenserian stanza
- Invented by **Edmund Spenser** for *The Faerie Queene*.
- **Nine lines**: eight lines of iambic pentameter + one final line of iambic hexameter (alexandrine).
- Rhyme scheme: **ABABBCBCC**.
- Effect: slow, stately, musical; the alexandrine rounds off the stanza.

### Terza rima
- Italian form: three-line stanzas with **interlocking rhyme**.
- Pattern: **ABA BCB CDC DED …**.
- Final line often rhymes with the first line to close the circle.
- Brought into English by **Shelley** (*Ode to the West Wind*) and **Dante** in Italian (*Divine Comedy*).
- Effect: continuous, forward-driving momentum.

## Metre, feet, and scansion

### Basic terms
- **Foot**: a unit of metre with one stressed + one or more unstressed syllables.
- **Iamb**: unstressed + stressed (da-DUM) — the most common foot in English.
- **Trochee**: stressed + unstressed (DUM-da).
- **Anapest**: unstressed + unstressed + stressed (da-da-DUM).
- **Dactyl**: stressed + unstressed + unstressed (DUM-da-da).
- **Spondee**: stressed + stressed (DUM-DUM), often for emphasis.

### Common line lengths
- **Dimeter**: 2 feet
- **Trimeter**: 3 feet
- **Tetrameter**: 4 feet
- **Pentameter**: 5 feet
- **Hexameter**: 6 feet (alexandrine)

### Iambic pentameter
- Five iambs per line: **da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM da-DUM**.
- Natural-sounding in English; used in sonnets, blank verse, heroic couplets.

### Scansion
- The analysis of a poem's metre.
- Mark stressed (/) and unstressed (∪) syllables.
- Not every line is perfectly regular; poets vary metre for effect.

## Rhyme and stanza comparison

| Form | Lines | Rhyme | Metre | Famous example |
|------|-------|-------|-------|----------------|
| Heroic couplet | 2 | AA | iambic pentameter | Pope, *Essay on Man* |
| Blank verse | any | none | iambic pentameter | Milton, *Paradise Lost* |
| Spenserian stanza | 9 | ABABBCBCC | 8 pentameter + 1 alexandrine | Spenser, *The Faerie Queene* |
| Terza rima | 3 (interlocking) | ABA BCB | usually iambic | Shelley, *Ode to the West Wind* |

### Exam checklist
- Heroic couplet = **2 rhyming lines, iambic pentameter**.
- Blank verse = **iambic pentameter, no rhyme**.
- Spenserian stanza = **9 lines, ABABBCBCC, final alexandrine**.
- Terza rima = **three-line interlocking rhyme (aba bcb cdc)**.
- Iambic pentameter = **five iambs per line**.
