# Nature and national income

## What is macroeconomics?

- **Macroeconomics** studies the economy as a whole: total output, employment, inflation, growth, and balance of payments.
- It deals with aggregates such as national income, aggregate demand, aggregate supply, and general price level.
- It helps governments design fiscal policy, monetary policy, and development plans.

### Micro vs macroeconomics

| Feature | Microeconomics | Macroeconomics |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Unit of study | Individual households, firms, industries | Whole economy |
| Key concerns | Price of a good, consumer choice, firm profit | National income, unemployment, inflation |
| Tools | Demand and supply curves | Aggregate demand and aggregate supply |
| Policy use | Market regulation | Fiscal and monetary policy |

### Central macroeconomic issues
- **Economic growth**: rise in real GDP over time.
- **Unemployment**: people willing and able to work but without jobs.
- **Inflation**: sustained increase in general price level.
- **Stability**: avoiding severe booms and recessions.

## Circular flow of income

### Basic idea
- Income, output, and spending flow continuously between households and firms.
- Households supply factors of production (land, labour, capital, enterprise) and receive income (rent, wages, interest, profit).
- Firms produce goods/services and sell them to households.

### Two-sector model
- Only households and firms; no government or foreign sector.
- Firms pay income to households; households spend it back on goods/services.
- Equilibrium condition: **Leakages = Injections**.

### Three-sector model
- Adds **government**: collects taxes and spends on public goods.
- Leakage: taxes; Injection: government spending.

### Four-sector model
- Adds **foreign sector**: exports and imports.
- Leakages: savings, taxes, imports.
- Injections: investment, government spending, exports.

### Leakages and injections

| Leakages (withdraw income from flow) | Injections (add spending to flow) |
|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| Savings (S) | Investment (I) |
| Taxes (T) | Government spending (G) |
| Imports (M) | Exports (X) |

## National income: key concepts

### Gross Domestic Product (GDP)
- Market value of all **final goods and services produced within a country's borders** during a year.
- Avoids double-counting by counting only final goods, not intermediate goods.

### Gross National Product (GNP)
- **GNP = GDP + Net factor income from abroad**.
- Includes income earned by residents overseas minus income earned by foreigners domestically.

### Net Domestic Product (NDP)
- **NDP = GDP − Depreciation**.
- Depreciation is also called consumption of fixed capital.

### Net National Product (NNP)
- **NNP = GNP − Depreciation**.
- Often treated as **national income at market prices** before adjustments.

### Market price vs factor cost
- **Market price**: includes indirect taxes, excludes subsidies.
- **Factor cost**: excludes indirect taxes, includes subsidies.
- **NNP at factor cost = NNP at market price − indirect taxes + subsidies**.

### Personal and disposable income
- **Personal Income**: income actually received by households.
- **Disposable Income**: personal income minus direct taxes; available for consumption and saving.

### Nominal vs real income
- **Nominal**: measured in current prices.
- **Real**: adjusted for inflation using a price index.
- Real values give a better picture of actual purchasing power.

## Measuring national income

### Three methods

| Method | What it measures | Formula |
|--------|------------------|---------|
| Income method | Sum of all factor incomes | Wages + rent + interest + profit |
| Expenditure method | Total spending on final goods | C + I + G + (X − M) |
| Product/value-added method | Sum of value added at each stage | GDP = gross value added |

### Difficulties in measurement
- **Non-market activities**: household work, barter, informal sector are hard to value.
- **Double counting**: must distinguish final from intermediate goods.
- **Depreciation**: estimating wear and tear is imprecise.
- **Illegal/unreported income**: shadow economy escapes official data.
- **Price changes**: require adjustment to real terms.
- **Externalities**: pollution and environmental damage are often ignored.

### Green accounting and welfare
- **Green accounting** adjusts national income for environmental costs and resource depletion.
- GDP growth does not automatically mean better welfare; it ignores inequality, health, leisure, and environment.
- **Human Development Index (HDI)** is a broader welfare measure.

## Exam checklist

- Macroeconomics = economy-wide aggregates.
- Circular flow: households ↔ firms; leakages (S, T, M) = injections (I, G, X).
- GDP = domestic production; GNP = GDP + net factor income from abroad.
- NDP = GDP − depreciation; NNP = GNP − depreciation.
- Three methods: income, expenditure, product/value-added.
- Nominal vs real: adjust for inflation.
- Green accounting includes environmental costs.
