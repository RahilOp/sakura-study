# Classical theory of employment

## The classical view of employment

- Classical economists (before Keynes) believed that a market economy automatically tends toward **full employment**.
- Their confidence rested on **Say's Law**, **wage-price flexibility**, and the equality of saving and investment.
- Full employment here means all willing workers are employed at the prevailing wage; some frictional/unemployment is allowed.

### Key classical assumptions
- Free markets adjust through price and wage changes.
- No general overproduction or deficiency of demand.
- Money acts only as a medium of exchange (classical dichotomy).
- Flexible wages and prices keep labour and product markets clear.

---

## Say's Law

### Statement
- **"Supply creates its own demand."**
- Production generates income (wages, rent, interest, profit), which becomes spending power.
- Every act of producing and selling also creates the ability to buy.

### Implication
- General gluts or economy-wide unemployment cannot occur.
- If one good is overproduced, another is underproduced; relative prices adjust.
- There can be no persistent deficiency of aggregate demand.

### Classical reply to unemployment
- If workers are unemployed, wages are too high.
- Wages will fall until all willing workers are hired.
- Similarly, unsold goods mean prices are too high; prices fall until markets clear.

---

## Wage-price flexibility and saving-investment equality

### Wage-price flexibility
- Classical theory assumes wages and prices are **flexible downward**.
- A fall in wages raises labour demand and reduces labour supply, restoring equilibrium.
- A fall in prices raises real spending and clears product markets.

### Saving-investment equality
- **Saving** is a leakage from the circular flow; **investment** is an injection.
- Classical economists argued saving is automatically converted into investment through interest-rate adjustments.
- If saving exceeds investment, interest rates fall; if investment exceeds saving, interest rates rise.
- Thus, leakages equal injections and full employment is maintained.

### Classical model summary

| Problem | Classical solution |
|---------|-------------------|
| Unemployment | Wages fall |
| Unsold goods | Prices fall |
| Excess saving | Interest rate falls, investment rises |

## The Keynesian critique

### Keynes's central argument
- **J. M. Keynes** rejected the idea that economies automatically reach full employment.
- In the short run, **aggregate demand** determines output and employment.
- If aggregate demand is insufficient, unemployment persists even with wage flexibility.

### Effective demand
- **Effective demand** = aggregate demand that actually becomes income/output.
- It depends on consumption and investment spending.
- A fall in investment or consumption can create a recessionary gap.

### Keynes vs. classical on wages
- Keynes: cutting wages during a recession may reduce workers' incomes and further lower demand.
- Wage flexibility may not cure unemployment if the problem is lack of demand.

### Role of government and market

| Classical view | Keynesian view |
|----------------|----------------|
| Market is self-regulating | Market can fail to maintain full employment |
| Government should not interfere | Government should manage aggregate demand |
| Supply determines demand | Demand determines output and employment |
| Saving automatically becomes investment | Investment depends on expectations and profit |

---

## Aggregate demand and aggregate supply

### Aggregate demand (AD)
- Total demand for goods and services in the economy.
- **AD = C + I + G + (X − M)**.
- A fall in AD leads to lower output and higher unemployment.

### Aggregate supply (AS)
- Total output firms are willing to produce at different price levels.
- In the short run, AS may be upward-sloping; in the long run, it can be vertical at full-employment output.

### Equilibrium
- Macroeconomic equilibrium occurs where AD = AS.
- Keynes focused on **short-run** situations where AD could be below the full-employment level.

---

## Exam checklist

- Classical theory assumes **full employment** via flexible wages/prices.
- Say's Law: **supply creates its own demand**.
- Saving and investment equalise through **interest-rate adjustments**.
- Keynes: **effective demand** determines employment.
- Keynes vs classical: government intervention vs laissez-faire.
- AD = C + I + G + (X − M); AS = total output supplied.
