Keynesian theory, consumption and investment

Keynesian consumption function

The psychological law of consumption

  • Keynes stated that as income rises, consumption also rises, but not by as much.
  • This is the fundamental psychological law of consumption.
  • It implies that richer economies save a larger share of income.

Average and marginal propensities

  • APC (Average Propensity to Consume) = C/Y.
  • APS (Average Propensity to Save) = S/Y.
  • MPC (Marginal Propensity to Consume) = ΔC/ΔY.
  • MPS (Marginal Propensity to Save) = ΔS/ΔY.
  • Important identity: MPC + MPS = 1 and APC + APS = 1.

Properties of the consumption function

  • Autonomous consumption: consumption even at zero income (financed by borrowing or past savings).
  • Induced consumption: consumption that rises with income.
  • The consumption curve has a positive slope but flattens as income rises.

Theories of consumption

Absolute income hypothesis (Keynes)

  • Current consumption depends mainly on current disposable income.
  • As income rises, APC falls.

Relative income hypothesis (Duesenberry)

  • Consumption depends on one's relative position in the income distribution.
  • Demonstration effect: people spend more to keep up with others.
  • Ratchet effect: consumption standards are easier to raise than to lower.

Permanent income hypothesis (Friedman)

  • Consumption depends on permanent income, not temporary fluctuations.
  • People smooth consumption over their lifetime.
  • Temporary windfalls are mostly saved.

Life-cycle hypothesis (Modigliani)

  • People plan consumption over their lifetime.
  • Saving is high during working years; dissaving occurs in retirement.
  • Wealth affects consumption.

Saving function

Relationship with consumption

  • Y = C + S.
  • Saving is income not consumed.
  • Saving curve rises with income because MPS is positive.

Factors affecting saving

  • Level of income.
  • Interest rates.
  • Wealth and inflation expectations.
  • Social security and pension systems.
  • Cultural attitudes toward thrift.

Investment function

Types of investment

  • Autonomous investment: independent of income level; influenced by interest rates, technology, expectations.
  • Induced investment: changes as income/output changes; rises in booms, falls in recessions.

Marginal efficiency of capital (MEC)

  • Expected rate of return on an additional unit of capital.
  • Investment is profitable when MEC > rate of interest.
  • MEC declines as more investment is undertaken (diminishing returns).

Factors determining investment

  • Rate of interest.
  • Business expectations and "animal spirits" (Keynes).
  • Technological change.
  • Government policy and tax incentives.
  • Capacity utilisation.

Investment and aggregate demand

  • Investment is the most volatile component of AD.
  • A small fall in investment can cause a large fall in income through the multiplier.

Consumption, saving, and equilibrium income

Determination of equilibrium

  • Equilibrium income occurs where planned saving equals planned investment: S = I.
  • If planned investment exceeds planned saving, income rises.
  • If planned saving exceeds planned investment, income falls.

Role of consumption in stability

  • High MPC makes the multiplier large but also means demand shocks spread quickly.
  • Low MPC makes the economy more stable but slows recovery.

Exam checklist

  • Keynes's psychological law: consumption rises with income, but less than proportionately.
  • MPC + MPS = 1; APC + APS = 1.
  • Four consumption hypotheses: absolute income, relative income, permanent income, life-cycle.
  • Autonomous investment is income-independent; induced investment varies with income.
  • MEC determines investment profitability.
  • Equilibrium income: S = I.
