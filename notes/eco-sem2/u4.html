Money, interest and IS-LM

The investment multiplier

Concept

  • The multiplier shows how a change in investment leads to a larger change in national income.
  • Formula: k = 1/(1 − MPC) = 1/MPS.
  • Higher MPC means a larger multiplier.

Why the multiplier works

  • Initial investment becomes income for households.
  • Households spend part of that income (MPC), creating further income.
  • The process continues until leakage through saving equals the initial injection.

Applicability in less developed countries (LDCs)

  • Multiplier may be smaller in LDCs because:
  • High MPC is offset by high imports (leakage).
  • Limited domestic production; extra demand raises imports.
  • Underemployment of resources increases the multiplier effect, but bottlenecks reduce it.
  • Conclusion: multiplier works best when the economy has unemployed resources and low import leakage.

The accelerator principle

Concept

  • The accelerator explains how a small change in consumption demand can cause a large change in investment.
  • Investment depends on the rate of change of output/consumption, not the absolute level.
  • Firms invest to expand capacity when demand rises rapidly.

Accelerator vs multiplier

Multiplier Accelerator
Investment → income Income/consumption change → investment
k = 1/MPS v = required capital per unit of output
Explains income expansion Explains investment fluctuations

Super multiplier

  • Combines multiplier and accelerator.
  • An increase in autonomous investment raises income; higher income raises induced investment; the process repeats.
  • Explains business cycles and cumulative growth.

Demand for money: liquidity preference

Keynes's three motives

  • Transactions motive: money held for daily purchases.
  • Precautionary motive: money held for unexpected needs.
  • Speculative motive: money held to take advantage of future changes in bond prices/interest rates.

Liquidity preference and interest rate

  • Demand for money is called liquidity preference.
  • The interest rate is the opportunity cost of holding money.
  • Higher interest rate → lower speculative demand for money.

Liquidity trap

  • At very low interest rates, people expect rates to rise and bond prices to fall.
  • They hold money rather than bonds; monetary policy becomes ineffective.
  • Associated with deep recessions.

The IS-LM model

What IS-LM explains

  • It shows simultaneous equilibrium in the goods market (IS) and the money market (LM).
  • Developed by J. R. Hicks and A. H. Hansen to interpret Keynes.

The IS curve

  • I = S (investment = saving) at each point.
  • Shows combinations of interest rate and income where the goods market is in equilibrium.
  • Downward-sloping: lower interest rates stimulate investment and raise income.

The LM curve

  • L = M (liquidity preference = money supply) at each point.
  • Shows combinations of interest rate and income where the money market is in equilibrium.
  • Upward-sloping: higher income raises transactions demand for money, requiring higher interest rates to maintain money-market equilibrium.

Equilibrium

  • General equilibrium is at the intersection of IS and LM.
  • This point gives the equilibrium interest rate and equilibrium income.

Shifts in IS and LM curves

Shifts in IS curve

  • Caused by changes in autonomous consumption, investment, government spending, or net exports.
  • Increase in G or I shifts IS right → higher income and higher interest rate.
  • Decrease shifts IS left.

Shifts in LM curve

  • Caused by changes in money supply or price level.
  • Increase in money supply shifts LM right → lower interest rate and higher income.
  • Decrease shifts LM left.

Policy implications

Policy Curve shifted Effect
Expansionary fiscal policy (G↑ or T↓) IS right Y↑, r↑
Contractionary fiscal policy IS left Y↓, r↓
Expansionary monetary policy (M↑) LM right Y↑, r↓
Contractionary monetary policy LM left Y↓, r↑

Exam checklist

  • Multiplier: k = 1/(1 − MPC).
  • Accelerator: investment depends on change in output.
  • Super multiplier combines multiplier + accelerator.
  • Liquidity preference: transactions, precautionary, speculative motives.
  • Liquidity trap: monetary policy ineffective at very low interest rates.
  • IS = goods-market equilibrium; LM = money-market equilibrium.
  • IS-LM intersection = general equilibrium income and interest rate.
