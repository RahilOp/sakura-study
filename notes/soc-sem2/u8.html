Change and transformation in Indian society

Culture: material and non-material

Material culture

  • Physical objects created by a society.
  • Examples: tools, technology, buildings, clothing, vehicles.
  • Changes rapidly with industrialisation and globalisation.

Non-material culture

  • Ideas, beliefs, values, norms, language, and customs.
  • Examples: religion, morality, family values, rituals.
  • Changes more slowly than material culture.

Cultural lag

  • A gap between rapid changes in material culture and slower changes in non-material culture.
  • Example: technology spreads quickly, but laws and values adjust more slowly.
  • Concept associated with William F. Ogburn.

Sources of social change

Internal factors

  • Technological innovation.
  • Economic development.
  • Education and literacy.
  • Social movements.
  • Legal reforms.

External factors

  • Colonialism and globalisation.
  • Migration and cultural contact.
  • Spread of media and communication technology.

Planned change

  • Government policies, five-year plans, and social reform movements.
  • Examples: land reforms, abolition of untouchability, family planning.

National integration

Meaning

  • National integration is the process of uniting diverse groups into a single national community.
  • It requires shared loyalty to the nation while respecting regional, linguistic, and religious identities.

Factors affecting national integration

Regionalism

  • Promotion of regional interests over national unity.
  • Can be cultural (language, identity) or economic (development demands).
  • Examples: demands for new states, river-water disputes.

Communalism

  • Use of religion for political purposes.
  • Creates hostility between religious communities.
  • Weakens secularism and national unity.

Naxalism

  • Left-wing extremist movement rooted in rural poverty, land alienation, and tribal marginalisation.
  • Affects several central and eastern Indian states.
  • Highlights failures of governance and development in remote areas.

Casteism

  • Discrimination and conflict based on caste.
  • Affects social harmony and equal opportunity.

Agents of national integration

Constitutional framework

  • Secularism, federalism, fundamental rights, and directive principles.

Democratic institutions

  • Parliament, judiciary, election commission, and civil services.

Shared symbols

  • National flag, anthem, Constitution, and national festivals.

Communication and transport

  • Railways, roads, media, and digital networks connect regions.

Common struggles

  • Freedom movement and shared historical memory.

Exam checklist

  • Material culture = physical objects; non-material culture = ideas and values.
  • Cultural lag = gap between material and non-material change.
  • National integration = unity amid diversity.
  • Regionalism, communalism, casteism, and Naxalism are challenges to integration.
  • Constitution, democracy, shared symbols, and communication promote integration.
