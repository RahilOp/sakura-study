# Population

## India's population: key facts

- India is the world's most populous country.
- Population size, growth, and composition are central to understanding Indian society.
- The Census of India collects population data every ten years.

---

## Determinants of population growth

### Birth rate
- Number of live births per 1,000 population in a year.
- India's birth rate has been declining but remains higher than many developed countries.

### Death rate
- Number of deaths per 1,000 population in a year.
- India's death rate has fallen due to better health care, sanitation, and nutrition.

### Migration
- Movement of people across regions or countries.
- Rural-to-urban migration is a major trend in India.
- Migration affects population composition in both source and destination areas.

### Fertility rate
- Average number of children born to a woman during her reproductive years.
- Declining fertility is a key feature of India's demographic transition.

---

## Demographic transition

### Stages
1. High birth rate and high death rate; slow population growth.
2. Death rate falls while birth rate remains high; rapid population growth.
3. Birth rate begins to decline; growth slows.
4. Low birth rate and low death rate; stable or ageing population.

### India today
- India is in the later part of stage 2 or early stage 3.
- Death rates are low; birth rates are declining but uneven across states.
- Kerala and Tamil Nadu have low fertility; some northern states have higher fertility.

---

## Age structure and dependency

### Young population
- India has a large proportion of young people.
- This can be an advantage if education and jobs are available.

### Dependency ratio
- Ratio of dependents (children under 15 + elderly over 64) to the working-age population.
- A falling dependency ratio means more potential workers relative to dependents.

### Demographic dividend
- Economic growth potential that arises from a large working-age population.
- Requires investment in education, skills, health, and job creation.
- If not managed well, youth unemployment can become a liability.

---

## Sex ratio and literacy

### Sex ratio
- Number of females per 1,000 males.
- India's sex ratio has historically been unfavourable to females due to son preference, neglect of girls, and sex-selective practices.
- The child sex ratio is a particular concern in some states.

### Literacy
- Proportion of people aged seven and above who can read and write.
- Literacy rates have improved but gender and regional gaps remain.
- Female literacy is strongly linked to lower fertility and better child health.

---

## Population policy

### Objectives
- Slow population growth.
- Improve health, education, and women's status.
- Ensure reproductive rights and choices.

### Measures
- Family planning programmes.
- Education and employment for women.
- Maternal and child health services.
- Awareness campaigns against child marriage and son preference.

### Debates
- Coercion vs voluntary choice.
- Focus on women's empowerment rather than population control.
- State-level variations in fertility and policy needs.

---

## Exam checklist

- Population growth depends on birth rate, death rate, and migration.
- Demographic transition: high birth/death → low birth/death.
- Demographic dividend requires education, skills, health, and jobs.
- Sex ratio = females per 1,000 males; India's is unfavourable.
- Literacy improves with education; gender gaps remain.
- Population policy should focus on empowerment and health, not coercion.
