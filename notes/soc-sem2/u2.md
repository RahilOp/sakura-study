# Perspectives to study Indian society

## Why use different perspectives?

- Indian society is complex; no single perspective explains everything.
- Different perspectives highlight different aspects: texts, history, institutions, functions, or conflicts.
- Exam focus: identify the perspective and what it emphasises.

---

## Indological perspective

### Main idea
- Studies Indian society through its **classical texts, religious traditions, and sacred literature**.
- Uses sources such as the Vedas, Dharmashastras, epics, and Puranas.

### Key thinkers
- **G. S. Ghurye** used Indological methods to study caste, kinship, and religion.
- **Irawati Karve** studied kinship and family using Sanskrit texts and fieldwork.

### Strengths
- Understands the cultural ideals Indians value.
- Useful for studying caste, dharma, karma, and family norms.

### Limitations
- May overemphasise textual ideal over lived reality.
- Can present a Brahmanical or upper-caste view.

---

## Historical perspective

### Main idea
- Studies Indian society by tracing institutions and changes over time.
- Looks at colonialism, nationalism, reform movements, and economic transformations.

### Key concerns
- How did British colonial rule reshape Indian economy, education, and caste?
- How did social reformers challenge sati, child marriage, and untouchability?
- How did independence and planning change rural and urban India?

### Strengths
- Shows continuity and change.
- Explains present problems through past processes.

---

## Structural perspective

### Main idea
- Examines how social institutions are arranged and how they relate to each other.
- Focuses on caste, class, family, religion, and the state.

### Key concepts
- **Social structure**: patterned arrangement of institutions and relationships.
- **Stratification**: hierarchical ranking of groups based on caste, class, gender, etc.
- **Institution**: established rules and practices that meet social needs.

### Thinkers
- **M. N. Srinivas** studied caste, village, and social change.
- **Andre Beteille** wrote on caste, class, and power.

---

## Functional perspective

### Main idea
- Sees society as a system of interrelated parts, each contributing to stability.
- Institutions exist because they perform necessary functions.

### Application to India
- Caste historically performed economic, political, and ritual functions in village life.
- Family provides socialisation, care, and economic support.
- Religion creates moral order and community solidarity.

### Limitations
- Tends to ignore conflict, power, and inequality.
- May justify existing social arrangements.

---

## Marxist/conflict perspective

### Main idea
- Society is shaped by economic inequality and conflict between classes.
- Social change comes from struggle over resources and power.

### Application to India
- Rural class conflict between landlords and landless labourers.
- Industrial conflict between capitalists and workers.
- Caste and patriarchy are linked to control over land and labour.

### Thinkers
- **D. P. Mukerji** and other Indian Marxist sociologists.

---

## Comparing perspectives

| Perspective | Focus | Key question |
|-------------|-------|--------------|
| Indological | Texts and traditions | What do classical sources say? |
| Historical | Change over time | How did the present emerge? |
| Structural | Institutions and stratification | How is society organised? |
| Functional | Stability and contribution | What function does each part serve? |
| Marxist | Conflict and inequality | Who controls resources and power? |

### Exam checklist
- Indological = texts and traditions.
- Historical = change over time.
- Structural = institutions and stratification.
- Functional = society as integrated system.
- Marxist = class conflict and inequality.
