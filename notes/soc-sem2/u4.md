# Tribal communities in India

## Who are the Adivasis?

- **Adivasi** means original inhabitant; it refers to the indigenous or tribal peoples of India.
- Tribal communities are listed in the Constitution as **Scheduled Tribes (STs)**.
- They have distinct cultures, languages, social organisation, and often close ties to land and forests.

### Population and distribution
- STs comprise about **8.6%** of India's population (2011 Census).
- Largest ST populations are in Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan, and Gujarat.
- Highest ST percentages are in Lakshadweep, Mizoram, Nagaland, Meghalaya, and Arunachal Pradesh.

## Livelihood patterns

### Traditional occupations
- **Shifting cultivation (jhum)**: slash-and-burn agriculture practised in Northeast and hilly regions.
- **Terraced farming**: in hilly areas.
- **Hunting-gathering**: among some forest-dwelling groups.
- **Pastoralism**: herding animals.
- **Settled agriculture**: many tribes have adopted plough agriculture.

### Forest dependence
- Many tribes depend on forests for food, fuel, medicine, and livelihood.
- Forest laws and conservation projects have often restricted their rights.
- The **Forest Rights Act, 2006** recognises tribal rights over forest land and resources.

---

## Issues facing tribal communities

### Land alienation
- Tribal lands have been lost to settlers, industries, dams, and mining projects.
- Often accompanied by debt bondage and exploitation.

### Displacement
- Large development projects have displaced millions of tribal people.
- Resettlement and rehabilitation have often been inadequate.

### Education and health
- Lower literacy rates compared to the general population.
- Poor access to health care, clean water, and sanitation.
- Malnutrition and disease are common in remote areas.

### Identity and culture
- Pressure to assimilate into mainstream society.
- Loss of language, ritual, and traditional knowledge.
- Threats from religious conversion and commercialisation.

---

## Constitutional safeguards

### Fifth Schedule
- Applies to scheduled areas in nine states.
- Provides special governance provisions; Governor has a key role in protecting tribal interests.

### Sixth Schedule
- Applies to tribal areas in Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura, and Mizoram.
- Provides for autonomous district councils with legislative, judicial, and executive powers.

### Other protections
- Reservation in education, jobs, and legislatures.
- Prohibition of transfer of tribal land to non-tribals in scheduled areas.
- Special development programmes such as tribal sub-plans.

### Debates
- **Assimilation**: tribes should adopt mainstream culture.
- **Integration**: tribes should participate in national life while retaining identity.
- **Isolation**: some argue remote tribes should be protected from outside contact.
