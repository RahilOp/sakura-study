# Social classes in India

## What is social stratification?

- **Social stratification** is the hierarchical arrangement of individuals and groups in a society.
- It creates unequal access to wealth, power, prestige, education, and opportunities.
- In India, stratification operates through **caste, class, gender, religion, and ethnicity**.

---

## Agrarian classes

### Landlords and big peasants
- Own large landholdings.
- Often lease land to tenants or employ labourers.
- Exercise political and economic power in villages.

### Middle peasants
- Own and cultivate medium-sized plots.
- May hire some labour during peak seasons.
- Important group in agrarian politics.

### Poor peasants and agricultural labourers
- Own very little or no land.
- Work as wage labourers on others' fields.
- Most vulnerable to debt, seasonal unemployment, and low wages.

### Changing agrarian structure
- Green Revolution benefited some regions and classes but increased inequality.
- Land reforms were uneven across states.
- Migration to cities and non-farm work is common among landless households.

---

## Industrial and urban classes

### Capitalist/bourgeoisie
- Own factories, businesses, and means of production.
- Includes industrialists, large traders, and corporate owners.

### White-collar workers
- Salaried employees in offices, schools, banks, and government.
- Middle-class status; often education-dependent.

### Proletariat/working class
- Sell their labour for wages.
- Includes factory workers, construction workers, and transport workers.

### Informal sector
- Large part of India's workforce.
- Unregulated, low wages, no job security, no social security.
- Includes street vendors, domestic workers, daily labourers, and small artisans.

---

## The middle class in India

### Characteristics
- Salaried professionals, government employees, small businessmen, educated youth.
- Values education, property ownership, and social mobility.
- Culturally influential despite being a minority in population terms.

### Role in society
- Driver of consumer markets.
- Aspirational group for lower classes.
- Politically vocal but fragmented.

---

## Social exclusion and inclusion

### Social exclusion
- The process by which individuals or groups are denied access to resources, rights, and opportunities.
- Based on caste, gender, religion, disability, or region.
- Examples: untouchability, discrimination in housing, denial of education.

### Social inclusion
- Policies and processes that bring excluded groups into mainstream society.
- Includes reservations, scholarships, self-help groups, and legal protection.

---

## Social mobility in India

### Channels of mobility
- **Education**: the most important route for individual mobility.
- **Reservation/affirmative action**: increases access for SCs, STs, OBCs.
- **Urbanisation and migration**: move from rural to urban areas for better opportunities.
- **Political power**: elected offices and movements can raise group status.

### Barriers to mobility
- Caste discrimination.
- Poor quality of public education.
- Regional inequality.
- Lack of capital and networks.

---

## Exam checklist

- Stratification = hierarchical arrangement based on caste, class, gender, etc.
- Agrarian classes: landlords, rich/middle/poor peasants, agricultural labourers.
- Urban classes: bourgeoisie, white-collar workers, proletariat, informal sector.
- Middle class: salaried professionals and small businessmen; culturally influential.
- Exclusion = denial of rights/opportunities; inclusion = policies to reverse it.
- Mobility channels: education, reservation, urbanisation, politics.
