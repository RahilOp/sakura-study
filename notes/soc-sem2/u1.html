Structure and composition of Indian society

India's geographical and social composition

  • India is a vast country with enormous regional, linguistic, religious, and ethnic diversity.
  • Society is composed of villages, towns, cities, tribes, castes, classes, religious communities, and minorities.
  • Understanding Indian society requires studying both structure (how parts are arranged) and composition (who the people are).

Rural and urban India

  • Village: small settlement based mainly on agriculture and primary relationships.
  • Town: larger than a village, with some non-agricultural occupations and markets.
  • City: large, heterogeneous population with specialised occupations and institutions.
  • Rural-urban continuum: the idea that village and city are not opposite categories but form a spectrum.

Tribes and weaker sections

  • Scheduled Tribes (STs): indigenous communities recognised by the Constitution for special protection.
  • Scheduled Castes (SCs): communities historically subjected to caste-based discrimination.
  • Other Backward Classes (OBCs): socially and educationally backward groups.
  • Minorities: religious and linguistic groups smaller than the majority.

Women in Indian society

  • Women form nearly half the population.
  • Issues include gender inequality, dowry, violence, unequal property rights, and low workforce participation.
  • Constitutional and legal safeguards exist, but implementation remains uneven.

Population profile

Population size and growth

  • India has the world's largest population.
  • Population growth is shaped by birth rates, death rates, and migration.

Key demographic indicators

  • Sex ratio: females per 1,000 males; India's has been historically unfavourable to females.
  • Literacy rate: proportion of people aged seven and above who can read and write.
  • Dependency ratio: ratio of dependents (children and elderly) to the working-age population.

Age structure

  • India has a large young population.
  • A large working-age population can create a demographic dividend if supported by education and employment.

Issues related to composition

Regional imbalances

  • Some regions are more developed than others in income, education, and infrastructure.
  • Migration from less-developed to more-developed regions creates both opportunities and pressures.

Social inequality

  • Caste, class, gender, and religion intersect to produce inequality.
  • Inequality affects access to land, education, health care, and political power.

National unity in diversity

  • Despite diversity, India shares a constitutional framework, democratic institutions, and cultural exchange.
  • The challenge is to maintain unity while respecting differences.

Exam checklist

  • Indian society includes villages, towns, cities, tribes, castes, classes, and minorities.
  • Rural-urban continuum explains the spectrum between village and city life.
  • SC, ST, OBC, and minorities are key categories in Indian social policy.
  • Demographic concepts: sex ratio, literacy, dependency ratio, demographic dividend.
  • Unity in diversity is a defining feature of India.
