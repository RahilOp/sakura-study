# Basic institutions of Indian society

## What is a social institution?

- A **social institution** is a stable set of rules, norms, and practices that meet basic human needs.
- Major institutions include **family, marriage, religion, caste, class, and democracy**.
- Institutions shape behaviour, identity, and life chances.

---

## Caste

### Main features
- **Ascribed status**: determined by birth, not achievement.
- **Endogamy**: marriage usually within one's own caste.
- **Hierarchy**: ranked occupational and ritual groups.
- **Commensality**: rules about who can eat with whom.

### Varna and jati
- **Varna**: classical four-fold division — Brahmin, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra.
- **Jati**: thousands of local caste groups with specific occupations and regional names.

### Changes in caste
- Urbanisation, education, and reservation have weakened some caste barriers.
- Caste remains important in marriage, politics, and social identity.
- **M. N. Srinivas** studied concepts of **sanskritisation**, **dominant caste**, and **secularisation**.

---

## Marriage

### Functions
- Regulates sexual relations and procreation.
- Establishes kinship networks and inheritance.
- Provides social and economic support.

### Forms in India
- **Monogamy**: one spouse at a time (most common).
- **Polygyny**: one husband, multiple wives (permitted for some communities historically).
- **Polyandry**: one wife, multiple husbands (rare, e.g. some Himalayan communities).

### Key rules
- **Endogamy**: marriage within a group.
- **Exogamy**: marriage outside a group, such as clan or gotra.
- **Dowry**: transfer of goods/money from bride's to groom's family.

### Changes
- Legal reforms: minimum age, consent, prohibition of child marriage and sati.
- Rising age at marriage, inter-caste marriages, and changing gender roles.

---

## Religion

### Role in Indian society
- Religion provides meaning, moral order, identity, and community.
- It influences festivals, dietary habits, family law, and politics.
- India is constitutionally secular; the state does not favour any religion.

### Major religions
- Hinduism, Islam, Christianity, Sikhism, Buddhism, Jainism, and others.
- Personal laws vary by religion for marriage, divorce, inheritance, and adoption.

### Religion and society
- Religious pluralism is a feature of Indian life.
- Communalism arises when religion is used for political mobilisation.

---

## Family

### Joint family
- Extended family living together, pooling resources, and sharing property.
- Common in rural India; declining in urban areas.
- Functions: economic cooperation, child care, elder care, socialisation.

### Nuclear family
- Parents and children living separately from extended kin.
- Increasing in cities due to migration and employment.

### Changes
- Rise of working women, smaller family size, and elderly living alone.
- Family continues to be a central institution for identity and support.

---

## Class

### Meaning
- Class refers to economic stratification based on wealth, income, occupation, and education.
- Unlike caste, class is more **achieved** than ascribed.

### Classes in India
- Upper class: industrialists, large landowners, professionals.
- Middle class: salaried employees, small businessmen, educated service holders.
- Lower class: agricultural labourers, informal workers, unemployed.

### Class and caste
- Class and caste often overlap but are analytically distinct.
- A person from a lower caste can be wealthy; an upper-caste person can be poor.

---

## Democracy

### India as a democracy
- India is the world's largest democracy.
- It has regular elections, a multi-party system, and universal adult franchise.

### Democracy and society
- Democracy has empowered marginalised groups through reservations and political representation.
- It provides a framework for managing diversity and conflict.
- Challenges include money power, criminalisation, and identity-based mobilisation.

---

## Exam checklist

- Social institutions: family, marriage, religion, caste, class, democracy.
- Caste: ascribed, endogamous, hierarchical; varna vs jati.
- Marriage: monogamy, endogamy, exogamy, dowry.
- Family: joint vs nuclear; functions include support and socialisation.
- Class: economic stratification; distinct from but overlapping with caste.
- Democracy: universal franchise, representation, managing diversity.
