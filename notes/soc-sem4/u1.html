Deviance, crime and juvenile delinquency

What is deviance?

  • Deviance refers to behaviour that violates social norms and expectations.
  • What is considered deviant varies across societies, groups, and time periods.
  • Deviance is relative: the same act may be deviant in one context but acceptable in another.

Examples

  • Wearing informal clothes to a formal ceremony.
  • Speaking loudly in a library.
  • Breaking religious taboos.

Sociological view

  • Deviance is not just individual wrongdoing; it is defined by social rules.
  • Powerful groups often define what counts as deviant.

What is crime?

  • Crime is deviant behaviour that is prohibited by law and punishable by the state.
  • All crimes are deviant, but not all deviance is criminal.

Types of crime

  • Violent crime: murder, assault, rape.
  • Property crime: theft, burglary, fraud.
  • White-collar crime: illegal acts by respectable persons in occupational settings.
  • Organised crime: structured criminal activity such as drug trafficking or smuggling.
  • Cyber crime: criminal activity using computers and networks.

Juvenile delinquency

Meaning

  • Juvenile delinquency refers to antisocial or criminal acts committed by minors.
  • The age of juvenile status varies by legal system.

Causes

  • Family breakdown, poverty, and neglect.
  • Peer pressure and deviant subcultures.
  • Lack of education and employment opportunities.
  • Substance abuse.
  • Influence of media and internet.

Responses

  • Juvenile justice systems focus on rehabilitation rather than punishment.
  • Counselling, education, vocational training, and family support.
  • Debate continues over whether serious juvenile offenders should be tried as adults.

Theories of deviance and crime

Durkheim: anomie

  • Rapid social change weakens norms, causing anomie (normlessness).
  • Anomie can lead to deviance and crime.

Merton: strain theory

  • Society teaches everyone to desire success but not everyone has equal means.
  • People may respond through conformity, innovation, ritualism, retreatism, or rebellion.

Sutherland: differential association

  • People learn deviant behaviour through association with others.
  • Criminal techniques and attitudes are transmitted in intimate groups.

Labelling theory

  • Deviance is created when society labels someone as deviant.
  • Once labelled, a person may internalise the label and continue deviant behaviour.

Social control

Formal social control

  • Carried out by authorised institutions such as police, courts, and prisons.
  • Based on laws and official sanctions.

Informal social control

  • Carried out by family, peers, religion, and community.
  • Based on norms, customs, and social pressure.

Exam checklist

  • Deviance = violation of social norms; crime = violation of law.
  • Juvenile delinquency = criminal/antisocial acts by minors.
  • Theories: anomie, strain, differential association, labelling.
  • Social control: formal (state) and informal (family, community).
  • White-collar crime and cyber crime are important modern categories.
