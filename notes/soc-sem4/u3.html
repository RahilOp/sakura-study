Structural problems: poverty, caste, religion, minorities

Structural problems

  • Structural problems are rooted in the way society is organised.
  • They affect large groups and are linked to inequality, discrimination, and power.
  • Examples: poverty, caste inequality, gender inequality, communalism, and minority marginalisation.

Poverty

Meaning

  • Poverty is the lack of income and resources needed to meet basic needs such as food, shelter, clothing, education, and health.

Types

  • Absolute poverty: inability to afford a minimum standard of living.
  • Relative poverty: low income relative to the average in society.

Causes

  • Unemployment and underemployment.
  • Low wages and lack of assets.
  • Lack of education and skills.
  • Discrimination based on caste, gender, or religion.
  • Rapid population growth in some regions.

Consequences

  • Malnutrition, ill health, and low life expectancy.
  • Poor educational outcomes.
  • Social exclusion and vulnerability.

Poverty measures

  • Poverty line based on calorie intake or consumption expenditure.
  • Tendulkar committee and Rangarajan committee estimates in India.
  • Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI) considers health, education, and living standards.

Caste inequality

Forms

  • Discrimination in education, employment, and housing.
  • Untouchability and social exclusion.
  • Violence against Dalits.

Responses

  • Constitutional abolition of untouchability.
  • Reservations for SCs/STs/OBCs.
  • Protection under the Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act.

Gender inequality

Forms

  • Lower female literacy and workforce participation.
  • Son preference and sex selection.
  • Violence against women: dowry deaths, domestic violence, sexual harassment.
  • Unequal property and inheritance rights.

Responses

  • Legal reforms: Dowry Prohibition Act, Domestic Violence Act, POCSO Act.
  • Women's reservation and self-help groups.
  • Beti Bachao Beti Padhao and other programmes.

Communalism

Meaning

  • Use of religion for political mobilisation.
  • Creates hostility between religious communities.
  • Weakens secularism and national unity.

Causes

  • Political manipulation of religious identity.
  • Economic competition between communities.
  • Historical grievances and rumours.
  • Lack of inter-community trust.

Consequences

  • Riots, violence, and displacement.
  • Polarisation of society.
  • Damage to economy and social harmony.

Minorities and backward classes

Minorities

  • Religious and linguistic groups smaller than the majority.
  • Concerns: security, education, representation, discrimination.
  • Constitutional safeguards: fundamental rights, minority educational institutions.

Backward classes

  • Socially and educationally backward groups.
  • OBC reservations aim to improve their access to education and employment.

Human rights violations

Meaning

  • Denial of basic rights and freedoms guaranteed to all human beings.
  • Examples: torture, discrimination, forced displacement, child labour, trafficking.

Protection

  • Constitution of India: fundamental rights and directive principles.
  • National Human Rights Commission (NHRC).
  • International conventions and treaties.

Exam checklist

  • Poverty: absolute vs relative; causes and consequences.
  • Caste inequality: discrimination, untouchability, reservations.
  • Gender inequality: education, employment, violence, property rights.
  • Communalism: religion used for politics; causes riots and polarisation.
  • Minorities and backward classes need constitutional safeguards.
  • Human rights violations are addressed by NHRC and fundamental rights.
