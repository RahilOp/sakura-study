# Corruption, cyber crime, drug addiction, suicide, terrorism

## Disorganisational problems in society

- Disorganisational problems weaken social order and institutions.
- They include crime, corruption, substance abuse, suicide, and terrorism.
- These problems often reflect deeper social, economic, and political tensions.

---

## Corruption

### Meaning
- **Corruption** is the misuse of public or private power for personal gain.
- Examples: bribery, embezzlement, nepotism, favouritism, kickbacks.

### Causes
- Weak enforcement of laws.
- Excessive bureaucracy and discretionary power.
- Low salaries and poor accountability.
- Political and electoral funding pressures.

### Effects
- Undermines public trust in government.
- Reduces investment and economic growth.
- Misallocates resources away from public welfare.

### Anti-corruption measures
- Transparency in governance.
- Right to Information (RTI).
- Lokpal and Lokayukta institutions.
- E-governance and digital payments.
- Stronger audit and enforcement agencies.

---

## White-collar crime

### Meaning
- Illegal acts committed by respectable persons in occupational roles.
- Examples: fraud, tax evasion, insider trading, corporate scams.

### Features
- Often hidden and complex.
- Perpetrators usually have high social status.
- Causes large financial losses but may receive less punishment than street crime.

---

## Cyber crime

### Meaning
- Criminal activities carried out using computers, networks, or digital devices.
- Examples: hacking, phishing, identity theft, online fraud, cyberstalking, ransomware.

### Challenges
- Rapidly changing technology.
- Cross-border nature of many crimes.
- Difficulty in tracing offenders.
- Need for cyber laws and digital literacy.

### Legal response
- India's **Information Technology Act** addresses cyber crimes.
- Cyber cells and forensic labs assist investigation.

---

## Drug addiction

### Meaning
- Compulsive dependence on psychoactive substances such as alcohol, opioids, cannabis, or synthetic drugs.

### Causes
- Peer pressure, stress, unemployment.
- Family dysfunction and trauma.
- Easy availability of drugs.
- Curiosity and rebellion among youth.

### Effects
- Health problems, mental illness, and overdose deaths.
- Family breakdown and financial ruin.
- Crime to support the habit.

### Responses
- De-addiction centres and counselling.
- Awareness and education programmes.
- Strict regulation of drug supply.

---

## Suicide

### Meaning
- The intentional taking of one's own life.
- A serious public health and social problem.

### Causes
- Mental illness, depression, and hopelessness.
- Financial distress, academic pressure, relationship problems.
- Social isolation and stigma.
- Farmer suicides linked to agrarian crisis.

### Prevention
- Mental health services and helplines.
- Reducing stigma around mental illness.
- Economic and social support.

---

## Terrorism

### Meaning
- Use of violence or threat of violence to create fear and achieve political, ideological, or religious goals.

### Features
- Targets civilians.
- Aims at publicity and psychological impact.
- Often linked to ethnic, religious, or separatist movements.

### Causes
- Political grievances and exclusion.
- Poverty, unemployment, and radicalisation.
- Ideological indoctrination.
- Weak governance and conflict zones.

### Responses
- Intelligence, law enforcement, and international cooperation.
- Counter-radicalisation and community engagement.
- Addressing root causes such as inequality and conflict.

---

## Exam checklist

- Corruption = misuse of power for private gain.
- White-collar crime = illegal acts by respectable professionals.
- Cyber crime = crime using computers/networks.
- Drug addiction = compulsive dependence on substances.
- Suicide causes include mental illness, economic distress, and social pressure.
- Terrorism = violence for political/ideological goals.
