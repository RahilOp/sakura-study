Unit 2

Adaptation: text to film

What is adaptation?

  • Adaptation is the process of turning a literary text into a film, play, or other media form.
  • Films must translate inner thoughts, descriptions, and time spans into visual and auditory elements.
  • Adaptations can be faithful, loose, or use the source as inspiration.

Key concepts

  • Fidelity: how closely a film follows the original text.
  • Interpretation: the adapter's creative reading of the source.
  • Medium specificity: film uses images, sound, editing, and performance rather than words.

Why study adaptations?

  • Compare how different media tell the same story.
  • Understand cultural and historical changes between source and adaptation.
  • Analyse directorial choices: setting, casting, music, dialogue.

William Shakespeare — The Comedy of Errors

Source text

  • A Shakespearean farce about two sets of identical twins separated at birth.
  • The twins — Antipholus of Ephesus and Antipholus of Syracuse, plus their servants Dromio of Ephesus and Dromio of Syracuse — cause confusion when they arrive in the same city.
  • Themes: identity, family reunion, mistaken identity, comic chaos.

Key features

  • Fast-paced action based on mistaken identity.
  • Roman/New Comedy roots (Plautus's Menaechmi).
  • Ends with reunion and resolution.

Gulzar's Angoor (1982)

Adaptation choices

  • Angoor relocates the story to contemporary India.
  • Two pairs of twins are separated in childhood and later create comic confusion in the same city.
  • The film uses Indian family settings, humour, and music.

Comparison

Aspect The Comedy of Errors Angoor
Setting Ephesus, ancient Greece Urban India
Twins Two Antipholus, two Dromio Two Ashok, two Bahadur
Tone Farce, wordplay Bollywood comedy, family humour
Cultural translation Classical references Indian middle-class life

Exam points

  • Angoor is a Hindi adaptation of Shakespeare's The Comedy of Errors.
  • Both rely on mistaken identity and twin confusion.
  • Gulzar's film translates the farce into an Indian cultural setting.

Analysing the adaptation

What is preserved?

  • The central device of twin confusion.
  • The theme of identity and reunion.
  • Farcical tone and fast-paced plotting.

What is changed?

  • Setting, language, names, and cultural references.
  • Addition of songs and romantic subplots typical of Hindi cinema.
  • Class and family dynamics are Indianised.

MCQ focus

  • Director of Angoor: Gulzar.
  • Source play: The Comedy of Errors.
  • Key device: mistaken identity / twins.
