Unit 4

Rabindranath Tagore — Kabuliwala

Context

  • Rabindranath Tagore was a Bengali poet, writer, and Nobel laureate.
  • "Kabuliwala" is one of his most beloved short stories.
  • It was originally written in Bengali and has been widely translated.

Plot

  • Rahmat, an Afghan fruit-seller (Kabuliwala), befriends a little Bengali girl named Mini in Calcutta.
  • He reminds Rahmat of his own daughter back in Afghanistan.
  • Rahmat is imprisoned for stabbing a man who cheated him.
  • Years later, he returns and meets Mini again, now grown up and about to be married.

Themes

  • Universal fatherhood: Rahmat's love for Mini mirrors his love for his own daughter.
  • Innocence and distance: children bridge gaps of nationality, language, and age.
  • Homesickness and exile: Rahmat longs for Kabul.
  • Humanity beyond boundaries: affection transcends cultural and geographical differences.

Style

  • Tender, simple narrative.
  • Strong emotional appeal.
  • Mini's childish chatter contrasts with Rahmat's homesickness.

Hemen Gupta's Kabuliwala (1961)

Adaptation

  • Hemen Gupta directed the Hindi film adaptation.
  • Balraj Sahni played the iconic role of Rahmat.

Cinematic choices

  • The film emphasises the emotional bond between Rahmat and Mini.
  • Songs and music heighten the pathos of separation and reunion.
  • Calcutta of the early 1960s is recreated visually.

Comparison

Aspect Short story Film
Medium Written prose Visual narrative, music, performance
Emotional focus Inner thoughts of narrator and Rahmat Facial expressions, songs, direct interaction
Setting Calcutta Calcutta in the 1960s
Climax Brief reunion at Mini's wedding Emotional visual reunion

Exam points

  • "Kabuliwala" is by Rabindranath Tagore.
  • Main characters: Rahmat (Kabuliwala) and Mini.
  • Film director: Hemen Gupta.
  • Actor known for the role: Balraj Sahni.
  • Theme: fatherly love across cultural distance.

Analysing the adaptation

What the film preserves

  • The central relationship between Rahmat and Mini.
  • The theme of exile and homesickness.
  • The emotional climax at Mini's wedding.

What the film adds

  • Songs and background score.
  • Visual richness of Calcutta.
  • Expanded scenes to suit film length.

MCQ focus

  • Tagore wrote "Kabuliwala."
  • Rahmat is an Afghan Kabuliwala.
  • Hemen Gupta directed the 1961 film.
  • Balraj Sahni played Rahmat.
