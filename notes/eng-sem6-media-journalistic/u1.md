# Unit 1

## Media and society

### What is media?
- **Media** are channels through which information, ideas, and entertainment reach large audiences.
- Types: print (newspapers, magazines), electronic (radio, television), digital (internet, social media), outdoor (posters, hoardings).

### Media and society
- Media shape public opinion and reflect social values.
- They act as a **watchdog** by monitoring government and powerful institutions.
- They help set the public agenda by deciding which issues receive attention.

### Functions of media
- Inform, educate, entertain, and persuade.
- Surveillance of the environment.
- Correlation of different parts of society.
- Transmission of culture and social norms.

---

## Principles of mass communication and journalism

### Mass communication
- Communication from one source to a large, heterogeneous audience through mediated channels.
- Features: large audience, anonymous receivers, rapid dissemination, feedback is delayed.

### Principles of journalism
- **Accuracy**: facts must be correct and verified.
- **Objectivity**: report fairly without personal bias.
- **Balance**: present multiple sides of a story.
- **Independence**: avoid conflicts of interest.
- **Accountability**: correct errors and take responsibility.

### Tools of gathering information
- Interviews, observation, surveys, documents, press releases, and digital research.
- Verification is essential before publication.

---

## Misinformation and disinformation

### Misinformation
- False or inaccurate information spread **without intent to deceive**.
- Example: sharing an outdated news story thinking it is current.

### Disinformation
- Deliberately false information spread **to deceive or manipulate**.
- Often used for political or commercial gain.

### Fake news
- False stories presented as news.
- Can be satire, misinformation, or deliberate propaganda.

### Combating false information
- Fact-checking websites.
- Media literacy education.
- Source verification.
- Responsible sharing habits.

---

## Social media and online journalism

### Social media
- Platforms like Facebook, X (Twitter), Instagram, YouTube, WhatsApp.
- Allow user-generated content and instant sharing.
- Challenges: echo chambers, trolling, privacy concerns, viral misinformation.

### Online journalism
- News published on digital platforms.
- Features: speed, interactivity, multimedia, 24-hour news cycle.
- Challenges: clickbait, loss of advertising revenue, need for constant updates.

### Cyber media basics
- Websites, blogs, podcasts, and digital newsletters.
- Requires understanding of SEO, analytics, and digital ethics.

## Written English for journalism

### Précis
- A summary that captures the main points of a passage in fewer words.
- Must be clear, accurate, and concise.

### Paragraph and essay
- Paragraph: unified group of sentences on one idea.
- Essay: structured argument with introduction, body, and conclusion.

### News reporting
- **5Ws and 1H**: Who, What, When, Where, Why, How.
- **Headline**: catches attention and summarises the story.
- **Lead**: opening paragraph with the most important information.
- Types of leads: summary lead, question lead, quotation lead, descriptive lead.

---

## Public speaking and radio

### Public speaking
- Process of addressing an audience effectively.
- Key elements: clarity, confidence, body language, voice modulation, audience awareness.

### Radio news
- News production for broadcast.
- Emphasis on clarity, brevity, and sound.
- Scriptwriting, reporting, and presentation differ from print because the audience listens only once.
