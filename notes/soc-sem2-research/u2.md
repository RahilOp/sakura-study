# Scientific approach and research methods

## Scientific study of social phenomena

### What makes research scientific?
- **Systematic**: follows a planned method.
- **Objective**: based on observable evidence, not personal bias.
- **Verifiable**: findings can be checked by others.
- **Generalisable**: results can apply beyond the specific study.
- **Cumulative**: builds on previous knowledge.

### Scientific method steps
1. Observation of a problem.
2. Formulation of hypothesis.
3. Data collection.
4. Analysis and interpretation.
5. Drawing conclusions.
6. Testing and replication.

---

## Objectivity and subjectivity

### Objectivity
- Research free from personal feelings, values, and biases.
- Aimed for in natural sciences and positivist social science.

### Subjectivity
- Influence of personal perspective and interpretation.
- In social science, complete objectivity is difficult because researchers study human beings.

### Balancing the two
- Researchers should be aware of their own biases.
- Reflexivity means examining how one's background affects research.

---

## Positivism and phenomenology

### Positivism
- Associated with **Auguste Comte**.
- Believes social science should use methods similar to natural science.
- Focuses on observable facts, measurement, laws, and prediction.
- Favours quantitative methods: surveys, experiments, statistics.

### Phenomenology
- Emphasises understanding social life from the actor's point of view.
- Focuses on meanings, experiences, and interpretations.
- Favours qualitative methods: interviews, observation, case studies.

### Comparison

| Positivism | Phenomenology |
|------------|---------------|
| Objective reality | Socially constructed reality |
| Quantitative | Qualitative |
| Explanation and prediction | Understanding and interpretation |
| Surveys, experiments | Interviews, observation |

## Sampling techniques

### Population and sample
- **Population**: entire group being studied.
- **Sample**: smaller group selected from the population.
- A good sample represents the population accurately.

### Probability sampling
- Every member has a known chance of selection.
- Types:
  - **Simple random sampling**: random selection.
  - **Stratified sampling**: population divided into subgroups, then sampled.
  - **Systematic sampling**: selecting every nth member.
  - **Cluster sampling**: selecting groups rather than individuals.

### Non-probability sampling
- Selection not based on chance.
- Types:
  - **Convenience sampling**: readily available participants.
  - **Purposive sampling**: chosen for specific characteristics.
  - **Snowball sampling**: participants refer other participants.

## Data collection techniques

### Primary data
- Collected directly by the researcher.
- Methods: questionnaire, interview, observation, experiment.

### Secondary data
- Already collected by others.
- Sources: census reports, government records, books, journals, newspapers.

### Questionnaire
- Structured set of written questions.
- Useful for large samples.
- Needs careful wording to avoid bias.

### Interview
- Face-to-face, telephone, or online conversation.
- Types: structured, semi-structured, unstructured.
- Allows probing and clarification.

### Observation
- Watching behaviour in natural or controlled settings.
- Types: participant and non-participant; structured and unstructured.

---

## Exam checklist

- Scientific research = systematic, objective, verifiable.
- Positivism = natural-science approach; phenomenology = understanding meanings.
- Sampling: probability vs non-probability methods.
- Primary vs secondary data.
- Questionnaire, interview, and observation are key data collection tools.
