Descriptive statistics

Purpose of descriptive statistics

  • Descriptive statistics summarise and describe the main features of a data set.
  • They help researchers see patterns without making broad conclusions about a larger population.
  • Two main types: measures of central tendency and measures of dispersion.

Measures of central tendency

Mean

  • The arithmetic average.
  • Formula: sum of all values divided by the number of values.
  • Most commonly used measure.
  • Affected by extreme values (outliers).

Median

  • The middle value when data are arranged in order.
  • If there is an even number of values, the median is the average of the two middle values.
  • Useful when data are skewed or have outliers.

Mode

  • The value that occurs most frequently.
  • A data set can have one mode, more than one mode, or no mode.
  • Useful for categorical data.

Comparison

Measure Best used when Weakness
Mean Symmetrical data Distorted by outliers
Median Skewed data Ignores exact values
Mode Categorical data May not exist or be useful

Measures of dispersion

Range

  • Difference between the highest and lowest values.
  • Simple but sensitive to outliers.

Quartile deviation

  • Half the difference between the third quartile (Q3) and first quartile (Q1).
  • Measures spread around the median.

Mean deviation

  • Average distance of each value from the mean.
  • Uses absolute values of deviations.

Standard deviation

  • Most widely used measure of spread.
  • Based on squared deviations from the mean.
  • Formula: square root of variance.

Variance

  • Average of squared deviations from the mean.
  • Units are squared, so less intuitive than standard deviation.

Coefficient of variation

  • Standard deviation expressed as a percentage of the mean.
  • Useful for comparing variability across different data sets.

Standard deviation in interpretation

Empirical rule (for normal distributions)

  • About 68% of values fall within ±1 standard deviation of the mean.
  • About 95% fall within ±2 standard deviations.
  • About 99.7% fall within ±3 standard deviations.

Skewness

  • Measures asymmetry of a distribution.
  • Positive skew: tail extends to the right; mean > median.
  • Negative skew: tail extends to the left; mean < median.

Kurtosis

  • Measures the peakedness or flatness of a distribution.
  • High kurtosis = sharp peak and heavy tails.
  • Low kurtosis = flat peak and light tails.

Exam checklist

  • Central tendency: mean, median, mode.
  • Dispersion: range, quartile deviation, mean deviation, standard deviation, variance.
  • Standard deviation is the most commonly used measure of spread.
  • Empirical rule: 68-95-99.7% within 1-2-3 SDs for normal data.
  • Skewness = asymmetry; kurtosis = peakedness.
