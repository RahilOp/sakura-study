# Correlation and Chi-Square tests

## Correlation

### Meaning
- **Correlation** measures the strength and direction of relationship between two variables.
- It does **not** prove causation.

### Direction
- **Positive correlation**: both variables increase together.
- **Negative correlation**: one variable increases while the other decreases.
- **Zero correlation**: no linear relationship.

### Strength
- Values range from **−1** to **+1**.
- **+1** = perfect positive correlation.
- **−1** = perfect negative correlation.
- **0** = no correlation.

### Pearson's correlation coefficient (r)
- Used for **interval/ratio** data.
- Measures linear relationship.
- Requires approximately normal distribution.

### Spearman's rank correlation (rho)
- Used for **ordinal** data or when assumptions of Pearson are not met.
- Based on ranked data rather than raw scores.

### Important caution
- **Correlation does not imply causation.**
- A third variable may explain the relationship.

---

## Interpreting correlation

| Value of r | Interpretation |
|------------|----------------|
| 0.00 to 0.19 | Very weak |
| 0.20 to 0.39 | Weak |
| 0.40 to 0.59 | Moderate |
| 0.60 to 0.79 | Strong |
| 0.80 to 1.00 | Very strong |

- Same scale applies to negative values.

## Chi-Square test

### Purpose
- Tests whether there is a significant association between two **categorical variables**.
- Non-parametric: does not assume normal distribution.

### Steps
1. State null and alternative hypotheses.
2. Create a contingency table of observed frequencies.
3. Calculate expected frequencies for each cell.
4. Compute Chi-Square statistic.
5. Compare to critical value or use p-value.

### Expected frequency
- **Expected = (row total × column total) / grand total**.
- Expected frequencies should generally be at least 5 in most cells.

### Degrees of freedom
- **df = (rows − 1) × (columns − 1)**.

### Interpretation
- A significant Chi-Square result means the variables are associated.
- It does not indicate the strength or direction of the association.

---

## When to use which test

| Situation | Test |
|-----------|------|
| Two interval/ratio variables, linear relationship | Pearson's r |
| Two ordinal variables or non-normal data | Spearman's rho |
| Two categorical variables, association | Chi-Square |
| Compare sample mean to population mean | One-sample t-test |
| Compare means of two groups | Independent t-test |

---

## Exam checklist

- Correlation measures relationship, not causation.
- Pearson's r = interval/ratio linear correlation.
- Spearman's rho = rank correlation for ordinal data.
- Chi-Square = test of association for categorical variables.
- Expected frequency ≥ 5 for most Chi-Square tests.
- df = (rows − 1) × (columns − 1).
