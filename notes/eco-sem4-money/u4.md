## Non-banking financial intermediaries

### Meaning
- **NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies)** provide financial services but do not hold a banking licence.
- They accept deposits (in some forms) and lend, invest, or lease.
- They cannot issue cheques or provide payment systems like banks.

### Types
- Equipment leasing companies.
- Hire-purchase finance companies.
- Loan companies.
- Investment companies.
- Infrastructure finance companies.
- Microfinance institutions.

### Role in economic development
- Provide credit to sectors neglected by banks.
- Support small businesses, transport, and consumer durables.
- Mobilise savings from households.
- Increase financial deepening.

### Regulation
- NBFCs are regulated by the **RBI** under the RBI Act.
- Different categories face different capital and prudential norms.

## Financial inclusion

### Meaning
- **Financial inclusion** means ensuring access to useful and affordable financial products and services for all individuals and businesses.
- Includes banking, credit, insurance, pensions, and payments.

### Why it matters
- Reduces poverty by giving poor households access to savings and credit.
- Supports micro and small enterprises.
- Brings people into the formal economy.
- Improves efficiency of welfare programmes.

### Government and RBI initiatives
- **Jan Dhan Yojana**: universal access to bank accounts.
- **Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana**: loans for small businesses.
- **SHG-Bank linkage programme**: self-help groups access bank credit.
- **Digital payments**: UPI, AePS, RuPay cards.
- **Financial Literacy Centres**.

## Non-performing assets (NPAs)

### Definition
- An asset becomes **non-performing** when interest or principal repayment is overdue for a specified period (typically 90 days for most loans).
- Classified as sub-standard, doubtful, or loss asset.

### Causes
- Poor project appraisal.
- Economic slowdown.
- Diversion of funds.
- Governance and management failure in borrowing firms.
- External shocks and policy uncertainty.

### Impact
- Reduces bank profitability.
- Erodes capital base.
- Limits fresh lending capacity.
- Threatens financial stability.

### Management and resolution
- **SARFAESI Act**: allows banks to recover secured loans without court intervention.
- **Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (IBC)**: time-bound resolution of stressed assets.
- **Asset Reconstruction Companies (ARCs)**: buy bad loans from banks.
- **Prompt Corrective Action (PCA)** framework for weak banks.

## Emerging challenges in banking

### Cyber security
- Rising digital banking increases risks of hacking, phishing, and fraud.
- Banks must invest in secure systems and customer awareness.

### Fintech
- Technology-driven financial services: mobile wallets, peer-to-peer lending, robo-advisors.
- Challenges traditional banking models.
- Requires balanced regulation to encourage innovation while protecting consumers.

### Mergers and consolidations
- Merging weak banks with stronger ones to improve efficiency and capital.
- Reduces number of public-sector banks.
- Aims to create larger, more competitive banks.

### Other challenges
- Financial literacy gaps.
- Climate and ESG risks.
- Competition from big tech companies.
- Balancing profitability with social objectives.
