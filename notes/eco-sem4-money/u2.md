## Supply of money

### Definition
- **Money supply** is the total stock of money circulating in an economy at a point in time.
- It includes currency with the public and demand deposits with banks.

### Measures in India (RBI)

| Measure | Components |
|---------|-----------|
| **M1** | Currency with public + demand deposits with banks + other deposits with RBI |
| **M2** | M1 + post-office savings deposits |
| **M3** | M1 + time deposits with banks |
| **M4** | M3 + total post-office deposits |

- **M1** is the narrowest and most liquid measure.
- **M3** is the broad money measure most commonly used.

### Determinants of money supply
- **Monetary base/high-powered money** (H).
- **Money multiplier** (m).
- Money supply = **H × m**.

## High-powered money and money multiplier

### High-powered money (H)
- Also called **monetary base**.
- **H = Currency with public (C) + Cash reserves of banks (R)**.
- It is "high-powered" because a given change in H creates a larger change in total money supply.

### Money multiplier (m)
- Shows how much the money supply expands for each unit of high-powered money.
- Formula (simple version): **m = 1 / required reserve ratio**.
- A lower reserve ratio means a larger multiplier.

### Factors affecting the multiplier
- Cash reserve ratio (CRR).
- Currency-deposit ratio (public preference for cash).
- Excess reserves held by banks.

### Classical vs Keynesian views of money supply
- **Classical view**: money supply is exogenous; changes in M mainly affect prices (neutrality of money).
- **Keynesian view**: money supply affects interest rates, investment, and output in the short run.

## Commercial banking system

### Nature and functions
- Commercial banks accept deposits from the public and advance loans.
- Functions:
  - Accepting deposits.
  - Advancing loans.
  - Credit creation.
  - Agency services (cheques, drafts, bill payments).
  - Investment in securities.

### Assets and liabilities

| Liabilities | Assets |
|-------------|--------|
| Demand deposits | Cash reserves |
| Time deposits | Loans and advances |
| Borrowings | Investments |
| Capital | Fixed assets |

### Credit creation
- Banks create deposits by lending out a portion of deposits.
- Process: deposit → keep reserves → lend → re-deposit → lend again.
- Total deposits created = Initial deposit × money multiplier.

### Limitations on credit creation
- Amount of cash reserves.
- Cash reserve ratio.
- Public preference for holding cash.
- Creditworthiness of borrowers.
- Central bank policy.

## Banking in India

### Nationalisation
- **14 major commercial banks nationalised on 19 July 1969**.
- Purpose: expand banking to rural and unbanked areas, support priority sectors.
- More banks nationalised in 1980.

### Banking sector reforms
- Liberalisation in the 1990s: new private-sector banks, foreign banks.
- Prudential norms, capital adequacy, and NPA classification.
- Technology adoption: ATMs, core banking, digital payments.

### Role of commercial banks in a developing economy
- Mobilise savings from scattered households.
- Provide credit to agriculture, industry, and trade.
- Support government development programmes.
- Create money and maintain payment systems.
