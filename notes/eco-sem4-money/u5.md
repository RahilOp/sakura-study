# Unit 5

## Introduction to public finance

### Meaning and scope
- **Public finance** studies the revenue, expenditure, and debt of the government.
- It examines how governments raise and spend money to achieve economic and social goals.
- Branches: public revenue, public expenditure, public debt, financial administration, and fiscal policy.

### Public vs private finance

| Feature | Private finance | Public finance |
|---------|----------------|----------------|
| Objective | Individual profit | Public welfare |
| Compulsion | Voluntary | Can use taxation (compulsory) |
| Borrowing | Limited by income/collateral | Can borrow against future taxes |
| Secrecy | Private | Public accountability |
| Elasticity | Less elastic | More elastic (can print money, raise taxes) |

### Principle of maximum social advantage
- Government should allocate resources so that the **marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost**.
- Expenditure should be pushed to the point where the benefit of the last rupee spent equals the sacrifice of the last rupee raised.
- Also called **Dalton's principle**.

## Classification of goods

### Private goods
- **Rival** and **excludable**.
- Example: a cup of coffee, a mobile phone.

### Public goods
- **Non-rival** and **non-excludable**.
- Example: national defence, street lighting.
- Markets tend to underprovide public goods; government must supply them.

### Merit goods
- Goods that society considers beneficial, even if individuals underconsume them.
- Example: education, healthcare, vaccination.
- Government may subsidise or provide them.

### Mixed goods
- Have features of both private and public goods.
- Example: roads, parks, public transport.

### Social goods
- Goods that promote social welfare broadly; sometimes used interchangeably with public and merit goods.

## Public revenue

### Sources
- **Tax revenue**: direct taxes (income tax, corporation tax) and indirect taxes (GST, customs duty).
- **Non-tax revenue**: fees, fines, dividends from public enterprises, grants.
- **Capital receipts**: borrowings, disinvestment proceeds.

### Canons of taxation
- Adam Smith's canons: equity, certainty, convenience, economy.
- Modern additions: productivity, elasticity, simplicity, diversity.

### Tax incidence
- **Impact**: initial burden on the person legally liable.
- **Incidence**: final burden after shifting.
- Taxes can be shifted forward to consumers or backward to suppliers.

## Public expenditure and debt

### Public expenditure
- Spending by government on goods, services, transfers, and subsidies.
- **Development expenditure**: roads, education, health, irrigation.
- **Non-development expenditure**: defence, interest payments, administrative costs.

### Public debt
- Borrowings by government to finance deficits.
- **Internal debt**: borrowed from domestic sources.
- **External debt**: borrowed from foreign sources.
- Too much debt can crowd out private investment and create interest burden.

---

## Budget and fiscal policy

### Budget
- Annual statement of estimated government receipts and expenditure.
- Presented by the Finance Minister in Parliament.

### Types of budgets
- **Balanced budget**: revenue = expenditure.
- **Surplus budget**: revenue > expenditure.
- **Deficit budget**: expenditure > revenue.

### Fiscal policy
- Use of government spending and taxation to influence the economy.
- **Expansionary fiscal policy**: increase G, decrease T → stimulates demand.
- **Contractionary fiscal policy**: decrease G, increase T → controls inflation.

### Fiscal policy for different goals
- **Inflation**: reduce government spending, raise taxes.
- **Unemployment/full employment**: increase spending, cut taxes.
- **Economic growth**: invest in infrastructure, education, and capital formation.

### Deficit financing
- Financing government deficit by borrowing from the central bank (printing money) or market borrowing.
- Can be inflationary if not matched by output growth.
- In developing countries, used to fund development when tax revenue is low.

## Exam checklist

- Public finance = government revenue, expenditure, and debt.
- Maximum social advantage = MSB = MSC.
- Public goods are non-rival and non-excludable; merit goods are socially beneficial.
- Taxes: direct vs indirect; impact vs incidence.
- Budget types: balanced, surplus, deficit.
- Expansionary fiscal policy fights recession; contractionary fiscal policy fights inflation.
- Deficit financing can be inflationary.
